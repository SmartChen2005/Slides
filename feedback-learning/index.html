6 / Critical evaluation

12 / 16

A useful synthesis.
An uneven evidence base.

What strengthens the review

A transparent process

Seven databases and reference searches.
Independent screening and quality ratings.
Study characteristics reported in detail.

What limits the conclusions

Quality and comparability

15 of 23 studies rated lower quality.
No study blinded outcome assessors.
Different tasks and combined interventions.

What remains unresolved?

The average benefit, optimal frequency, long-term retention, and which feedback suits which learner.

Paper §§4.4–5 · Evaluation combines author limitations and presenter critique

6 / What counts as learning?

13 / 16

Does the improvement remain
when the feedback disappears?

During practice

Performance

Can the learner do better

After a delay

Retention

Can they still perform

In another setting

Transfer

Can they use the skill

Presenter’s critique: the review does not systematically separate these outcomes. Durable learning needs more than a better practice score.

7 / Connection to XR

14 / 16

XR can test feedback designs.
It cannot assume they work.

Replay a movement

Compare visual replay with the same spoken guidance.
Video evidence does not establish the benefit of 3D overlays.

Pair encouragement with a cue

Give a concrete next action.
Praise findings do not directly establish benefits from badges.

Vary when guidance appears

Test frequency or learner-requested feedback.
Feedback benefits do not imply feedback on every attempt.

Design hypotheses beyond the paper’s direct evidence. Evaluate tracking accuracy, independent performance, retention, and transfer.

Presenter’s XR design hypotheses, informed by paper §§4–5

8 / A concrete application

15 / 16

An AR throwing coach,
with a testable learning claim.

After an attempt

Replay. Encourage.
Give one movement cue.

Show the throw alongside an expert example.
“Good effort. Step forward with your opposite foot.”

Proposed prototype, not a tested system in the review.

Compare equal practice time
  1. Praise alone
  2. Praise + movement information
  3. The same information + visual replay

Then test without assistance:
accuracy · delayed retention · real throws

The comparison isolates the added value of information and replay. Improvement in the headset is only the first outcome.

Proposed application and evaluation design · Not an intervention tested by this paper

Takeaways
