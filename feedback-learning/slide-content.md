# Slide 1

## Effects of Feedback on Students’ Motor Skill Learning in Physical Education: A Systematic Review

Does feedback help students learn movement skills—and which kinds help most?

Yulan Zhou · Wei De Shao · Lijuan Wang

2021 · International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health

# Slide 2

## Why motor skill learning matters in school PE

### Participation in physical activity

Children with better motor skills are more likely to participate in physical activity.

### Links to physical fitness

Motor skill development is associated with cardiorespiratory fitness, muscular endurance, and strength.

### School PE as a learning setting

School PE provides opportunities to develop motor skills, a central goal of the curriculum.

Given the importance of motor skills, which teaching strategies help students learn them? This review focuses on feedback.

# Slide 3

## Does feedback help?

### Mixed findings

Some studies found that feedback improved students’ motor skills. Others found no significant difference.

### Different feedback designs

Spoken guidance, video replay, praise, and corrective comments provide different kinds of feedback. Their effects may differ.

### Two research questions

1. Does feedback improve motor skill learning in school PE compared with no feedback?
2. How do feedback format and content affect learning?

This review brings together existing studies to assess the strength of the evidence.

# Slide 4

## What is feedback?

Feedback gives learners information about their performance. It may help them identify errors, adjust movements, and stay motivated to practise.

### Provider: Who gives it?

A teacher, a peer, or a video-based system.

### Format: How is it delivered?

Spoken comments, written comments, gestures, or visual feedback such as video.

### Content: What does it communicate?

- **Praise:** “Good effort.”
- **Movement information:** “Your elbow was below shoulder height.”
- **Correction:** “Raise your elbow on the next attempt.”

One feedback message can combine praise, movement information, and correction.

# Slide 5

## Why might feedback needs differ?

### Task demands

- **Movement:** Discrete (throw), continuous (swimming), serial (gymnastics routine).
- **Perception:** Open (changing conditions) vs. closed (stable conditions).

### Learner experience

**Fitts and Posner’s stages of skill learning**

- **Cognitive:** Understanding the movement.
- **Associative:** Refining technique and consistency.
- **Autonomous:** Performing with little conscious attention.

**Possible feedback needs**

- **Beginners:** Instructions on how to move.
- **Experienced learners:** Specific cues about errors.

*Possible explanations, not established rules.*

# Slide 6

## What this review adds

### 1. PE-specific synthesis

Pulls together 23 intervention studies of feedback in school PE classes across 11 countries.

### 2. Element-level evidence

Splits feedback into format and content, and grades the strength of evidence for each comparison.

### 3. Guidance for teachers

Give feedback regularly; combine praise with specific information rather than praise alone.

### 4. A research agenda

Calls for high-quality RCTs and studies of feedback frequency and technology-delivered video feedback.
